# Release watch — script, shot list and recording notes

The 1.3 video. It is **not** a features roundup, and it is not a sequel to the first
one. It has its own hook, because the audience is a different person.

The first video was for someone who **missed a show that was sold out**. This one is for
someone who **missed the booking window entirely** — who found out a film was on sale
from a friend's WhatsApp message, three hours late, when the only seats left were row
two at the edge.

---

## The one idea the video has to land

> Booking opens at a moment nobody announces, often days before release — and by the time
> your group chat finds out, the good shows are gone.

Everything else is a consequence. If a viewer takes only that away and never installs
anything, the video worked: it is true, most people have lived it, and it is worth
saying with no product attached. That is what makes it travel.

**Do not** try to also re-explain held seats being released before showtime. That was the
last video's idea, it is a good one, and stacking two revelations halves both.

---

## The first three seconds decide everything

Open on a WhatsApp-style group chat, not on BookMyShow. Everyone recognises the exact
moment: someone posts *"booked!"* and you didn't know it was even open. That image does
the work before a word is spoken.

---

## Master cut — shot list

Timings are cumulative. **On screen** is a burned-in caption — most people watch muted,
so captions carry the whole thing alone.

### 0:00–0:07 — Cold open. The hook.

**Shot.** A group chat on a phone. A friend's message: *"[Coolie] booked 🎉 first day
first show"*. Timestamp visible. Then your own reply typing out: *"wait it's open??"* —
sent. Then the reply: *"since morning bro"*.

**VO:** "I found out [Coolie] was on sale from a friend." *(beat)* "Nine hours late."

**On screen:** `I found out it was on sale from a friend.`
→ `Nine hours late.`

> Use a real chat with a real film, blurred names. A mocked-up chat reads as mocked-up
> instantly and this is the entire premise. If you have the actual message from the time
> you missed one, use that — nothing you write will be as good.

### 0:07–0:15 — The turn. This is the beat people will share.

**Shot.** BookMyShow's upcoming-movies list, scrolled slowly. No extension visible. Cut
to the same film's page, now with **Book tickets** on it.

**VO:** "Booking doesn't open on release day. It opens whenever the distributor says so —
often days early, at no particular hour, with no announcement. The page just changes. And
for a first-day-first-show, the good rows are gone inside the hour."

**On screen:** `Booking opens days early. At no fixed time.`
→ `Nothing announces it. The page just changes.`
→ `For a big release, the good rows go in under an hour.`

> The most valuable eight seconds in the video. Don't rush it, don't score it loudly, and
> don't cut to any UI during it. Let it be a fact about the world, not a setup for a demo.

### 0:15–0:23 — One click, weeks early

**Shot.** The upcoming-movies list again — now every poster carries a small bell in its
corner. Cursor moves to one, clicks. Bell turns to a ✓. A toast confirms.

**VO:** "So watch the film, not the showtime. Every film on the upcoming list picks up a
bell. Click it — weeks before there's anything to book."

**On screen:** `The + needs a showtime to exist. The bell doesn't.`
→ `Set it weeks early.`

> This is the shot that sells the release. Frame the bell tight enough to be unmistakable.
> The contrast with the first video's **+** is the point — say it out loud in the caption.

### 0:23–0:33 — Your cinemas, not all of them

**Shot.** Settings → the **Release watch** tab. The theatre list, two or three cinemas
ticked. The counter at the bottom reading `97 cinemas · 3 picked`.

**VO:** "Pick the cinemas you'd actually go to, and you're told which of them opened —
not that it's on sale somewhere in the city."

**On screen:** `Pick your cinemas. Get told which one opened.`

> Tick cinemas you genuinely would go to. Somebody local will notice if the list is
> nonsense for the city on screen.
>
> If you want one extra line for the technically-minded, this is where it goes: a film is
> listed several times over — one entry per language and format — and picking cinemas
> matches all of them at once, so a Telugu showing doesn't slip past a watch set from the
> Tamil listing. Cut it from the master if it costs you pace; it belongs in the YouTube
> description regardless.

### 0:33–0:45 — Everyone finds out at once

**Shot.** A phone, face up, dark. It lights up: a Telegram notification in a group —
**Booking open — [Coolie]**, then the cinema names, then a blue **Book now** button.
Thumb taps it. BookMyShow's showtimes open on the phone.

**VO:** "One person runs it. The whole group gets told the moment it opens — and taps
straight through to book."

**On screen:** `One person runs it. Everyone gets told.`
→ `Tap Book now. You're on the page.`

> Film this on a real phone, in one take, thumb included. A screen recording of the same
> thing is noticeably worse — the physical tap is what makes it read as *"I could have
> been the one who booked."*
>
> Use a real group with real friends' names blurred. The alert is genuinely from the
> extension; do not fake the message.

### 0:45–0:53 — The trust beat

**Shot.** Settings, scrolled slowly. The check interval. The "start checking 7 days before
release" field. Then the popup showing a watch reading `Starts checking in 12 days`.

**VO:** "There's no server. It runs in your browser, and it doesn't touch BookMyShow until
a week before release — because nothing opens months ahead."

**On screen:** `No server. No account. Nothing stored anywhere but your browser.`
→ `Sleeps until the release is close.`

> Say the dormancy line. It answers the question a technical viewer is already forming —
> *"so it hammers their site for three months?"* — before they can post it as a comment.

### 0:53–1:00 — Card

**Shot.** Static end card. Name, one line, store link.

**On screen:** `Seat Watch for BookMyShow`
→ `Free. Open source. Chrome Web Store.`

## Short cut (9:16, 18–22s) — Reels / Shorts / status

Three beats, no trust beat, no settings.

| | |
|---|---|
| **0:00–0:06** | Group chat: *"booked!"* → *"wait it's open??"* → *"since morning bro"*. Caption: `I found out from a friend. Nine hours late.` |
| **0:06–0:11** | Upcoming list, bell clicked, turns ✓. Caption: `Watch the film, not the showtime.` |
| **0:11–0:18** | Phone lights up with the group alert. Thumb taps **Book now**. Caption: `Everyone gets told the moment it opens.` |
| **0:18–0:22** | End card. |

Vertical is the cut that will actually get watched. Shoot the phone shots vertically from
the start rather than cropping the master — a cropped 16:9 phone shot looks like a
cropped 16:9 phone shot.

## Loop / GIF (6–8s, silent)

Bell clicked on the upcoming list → cut → phone lights up with the alert → cut back to the
bell. No captions, no faces, no text to read. This is the one that goes in a reply when
somebody asks *"what does it do?"*

## Recording setup

Same as [`demo-script.md`](demo-script.md) — 1280×720 logical, one clean Chrome profile,
cursor visible, no bookmarks bar, no other extensions. Two additions:

**The phone shots.** Wipe the notification shade first. Do Not Disturb on, then allow
Telegram through it, so nothing else lands mid-take. Shoot the tap in one continuous
motion; a cut between the notification and the tap kills it.

**The one shot you cannot stage honestly.** A booking window opening is a real event on
someone else's schedule, and you cannot summon one for a shoot. Two honest options:

1. **Wait for one.** Set a watch on a film releasing this week and film the alert when it
   genuinely arrives. Best footage, worst scheduling.
2. **Re-film a real alert.** Use one you actually received — the message is real, only
   the camera is late. Scroll back to it in the group and shoot that.

What not to do is compose a fake Telegram message. Somebody will spot the details,
and the whole video is built on the claim that this really happens.

## Which idea belongs where

The two videos are not a series, and they should not be posted as one.

| | Video 1 | Video 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | sold out isn't final | you found out too late |
| Feeling | *I could have gone* | *I could have booked* |
| Button | the **+** on a showtime | the **bell** on a film |
| Audience | anyone who missed a show | anyone in a film group chat |

Post them weeks apart, each on its own. Video 2 does not need video 1 to make sense, and
saying "in part one…" costs you every viewer who hasn't seen it.
