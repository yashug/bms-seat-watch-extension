# Demo video — script, shot list and recording notes

Three cuts, one recording session. Record the **60-second master** once and cut the other
two out of it; don't shoot them separately or the framing drifts.

| Cut | Length | Ratio | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Master** | 55–65s | 1280×800 (16:10) | Chrome Web Store listing video, YouTube, Product Hunt, X, LinkedIn |
| **Short** | 18–22s | 1080×1920 (9:16) | Reels, Shorts, WhatsApp status |
| **Loop** | 6–8s, no audio | 1280×800 or square | The GIF in the X post, the Reddit post, the README |

---

## The one idea the video has to land

Not "here is a Chrome extension with settings." The video has one job:

> **A show says SOLD OUT. Minutes later, four seats together — dead centre — and you got
> told before anyone refreshed the page.**

Everything that isn't that is trim. Nobody watches a 60-second demo for the options page.

## The first three seconds decide everything

You get **three seconds** before someone scrolls. Do not spend them on a logo, a title card,
or the word "Introducing." Spend them on a claim the viewer doesn't believe.

The cold open below is a **spoken line over a held shot** — no product, no branding, no UI
chrome. The extension does not appear until 0:12, and that's deliberate: by then the viewer
has already agreed the problem is real, so the product arrives as the answer rather than as
an ad.

**Openers, pick one and commit** (the shot list uses #1):

1. *"I missed [Interstellar] at [IMAX]. Not because it sold out."* — best if you have a real
   story. The pause after "sold out" is the whole hook.
2. *"Cinemas don't sell out. They stop offering."* — best if you don't. Punchy, impersonal,
   works muted.
3. *"Same film. Two screens. Not the same film."* — the film-buff cut. Use this one if
   you're posting to film communities rather than tech ones; it's a different audience and
   a better hook for them.

> Swap every `[bracketed]` detail for what actually happened to you. An invented story is
> the fastest way to get caught, and a real one is better anyway.

## Master cut — shot list

Timings are cumulative. Voiceover is the spoken line; **On screen** is a burned-in caption
(large, bottom third, 2s minimum — most people watch muted, so the captions have to carry
the whole thing on their own).

### 0:00–0:06 — Cold open. The hook.

**Shot.** BookMyShow, a big release, opening weekend. The showtimes list, scrolled slowly.
Several chips greyed out. Cursor hovers one sold-out chip and doesn't click. No UI, no
extension, no branding — just the grey.

**VO:** "I missed [Interstellar] at [IMAX]." *(beat)* "Not because it sold out."

**On screen:** `I missed [Interstellar] at [IMAX].`
→ `Not because it sold out.`

> Hold the beat after "sold out" for a full second before the next line. That pause is
> doing more work than any other frame in the video — it's where the viewer goes *"…what?"*
> Cut it tight and the hook evaporates.
>
> Pick a real film in a real cinema near you. A staged listing reads as staged instantly,
> and this shot is the entire premise. If nothing is genuinely sold out on the day you
> record, wait for a day when it is.

### 0:06–0:12 — The turn. This is the beat people will share.

**Shot.** Still on the grey listing. Slow push in on one sold-out chip.

**VO:** "Because it *un*-sold-out at 7:12pm on a Friday, and I wasn't looking. Cinemas hold
seats back — distributors, staff, corporate blocks. When nobody claims them, they go back
on sale. Usually one to three hours before the show. And nothing announces it."

**On screen:** `It un-sold-out at 7:12pm.`
→ `Cinemas hold seats back — and quietly release them 1–3 hrs before showtime.`
→ `Nothing announces it. The page just changes.`

> This is the most valuable six seconds in the video. It's genuinely new information to
> most people, it's true, and it's shareable *without* the product attached — which is
> exactly why the video travels. Don't rush it and don't undercut it with music.

### 0:12–0:20 — One click to watch

**Shot.** Cursor moves to the **+** in the corner of a *sold-out* showtime chip. Click.
The chip's + turns into a ✓. Then click the extension icon — the popup opens with that
show now in the list, film, cinema, screen and start time already filled in.

**On screen:** `Click the + on any showtime. Including the sold-out ones.`

**VO:** "Every showtime on BookMyShow picks up a small plus. Click it — including on the
sold-out ones, those are the ones worth watching."

> This is the shot that sells the extension: your **+** button sitting on BookMyShow's own
> page. Frame it tight enough that the + is unmistakable. Consider a subtle zoom-in on the
> chip for the click, then back out.

### 0:20–0:32 — What counts as a match

**Shot.** In the popup, the miniature hall: pale grey taken, slate blue free, and the green
run joined by a band. Then cut to the criteria — **seats together**, **where in the hall**,
**rows to skip at the front** — and change "seats together" from 2 to 4 on camera.

**On screen:** `Not "a seat is free." The seats you'd actually buy.`
→ `4 together · middle of the hall · not the front rows`

**VO:** "It isn't watching for *a* free seat. It's watching for four together, no aisle
between them, in the part of the hall you'd actually sit in. Four in the front corner and
four dead centre are the same price — and only one of them is worth leaving the house for."

> **If you're cutting a version for film communities**, replace this VO with the screen
> angle, which lands much harder there: *"Because I don't want a ticket. I want that
> screen. The same film in a worse room isn't the same film — so 'sold out' there is worth
> waiting on rather than settling around."* Same footage, different line. Worth exporting
> both; it's five minutes in the edit and doubles where the video works.

> The green band across the block is the strongest single frame in the whole video — four
> green dots *joined* reads as "four together" instantly. Give it a full beat before cutting.

### 0:32–0:44 — The alert

**Shot.** Cut away to a desktop with the browser behind other windows. The macOS/Chrome
notification slides in: the film, the cinema, and the block it found. Cursor clicks it.
Chrome comes forward on the BookMyShow **seat map**, with the block visible.

**On screen:** `Desktop notification. Click it, land on the seat map.`
→ `Optionally Telegram, Discord, or ntfy on your phone.`

**VO:** "When a matching block appears you get a notification — click it and you're on the
seat map. It can also reach you on Telegram, or a Discord or ntfy webhook, if you'd rather
be told on your phone."

> Staging this honestly: don't fake the notification. Set **seats together** to 2 on a show
> that genuinely has availability, hit **Check now**, and a real alert fires within seconds.
> That is the product working, just aimed at an easy target. If you want the "sold out →
> release" version you have to actually catch one — worth doing once and keeping the footage
> forever.

### 0:44–0:55 — The trust beat

**Shot.** Slow pan of the popup: countdown to showtime, the next-check bar filling, the free-
seats-over-time line, the blocks by row and price.

**On screen:** `No server. No account. Nothing leaves your browser.`
→ `It tells you. You book.`

**VO:** "There's no server behind this and no account to make. Your watch list lives in your
own browser and the checks run in your own Chrome. It doesn't book anything for you — it
tells you, and you decide."

### 0:55–1:00 — Card

**Shot.** Static end card. Icon, name, one line, the store link.

**On screen:**
```
Seat Watch for BookMyShow
Free · Chrome Web Store
Search "Seat Watch" on the Chrome Web Store
```

**VO:** "Seat Watch for BookMyShow. Free, on the Chrome Web Store."

> Do **not** burn the raw store URL into the frame. It's 96 characters ending in a
> 32-character extension id — unreadable at any size and impossible to type from memory,
> which is the only thing a URL on screen is for. Either point people at the search term,
> or register a short link (`seatwatch.link`, a Bitly, a GitHub Pages redirect) and use
> that. The real URL goes in the description, where it's clickable:
>
> `https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/seat-watch-for-bookmyshow/hkbeaeicmbnldhgfkoonkkebdlphnohn`

## Short cut (9:16, 18–22s) — for Reels / Shorts / status

Same footage, cropped to the action, no VO needed — captions carry it. Music: something with
a beat drop you can land the notification on.

| Time | Shot | Caption |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Grey sold-out chips, slow push in | `I missed [Interstellar] at [IMAX].` → `Not because it sold out.` |
| 0:03–0:07 | Still on the grey | `It UN-sold-out at 7:12pm.` → `Nobody tells you.` |
| 0:07–0:10 | Click the + on a sold-out chip | `Cinemas release held seats 1–3 hrs before showtime.` |
| 0:10–0:15 | Popup: hall + green band, criteria set to 4 | `Now it watches. 4 seats together, where you'd actually sit.` |
| 0:15–0:19 | Notification slides in, click through to seat map | `📱 ping.` |
| 0:19–0:22 | End card | `Seat Watch — free on Chrome` |

The vertical cut keeps the hook and drops the trust beat — on Reels nobody is asking about
your backend at second nine, they're deciding whether to keep watching at second two. The
"un-sold-out" caption is the retention beat; it's the one that stops the scroll.

Vertical framing: crop to the popup for the middle section — it's already a narrow column,
so it fills 9:16 far better than the BookMyShow page does. For the BookMyShow shots, crop to
the chips column rather than shrinking the whole page.

## Loop / GIF (6–8s, silent, no captions needed)

Just this: **+ clicked on a sold-out chip → cut → notification appears → cut → green band in
the hall.** Loop it. This is what goes in the X post and at the top of the Reddit post; it
has to be readable at 400px wide in a timeline, so crop hard and skip anything with body text.

## Recording setup

**Before you hit record**

- [ ] **Sign out or blur your account.** BookMyShow's header shows your name; the seat map
      page can show your city and profile. Record signed in (you have to be), then blur the
      header in the edit, or use an account whose name you don't mind publishing.
- [ ] Clean Chrome profile. No other extensions visible, no bookmarks bar (`⌘⇧B`), no other
      tabs, zoom at exactly 100% (`⌘0`).
- [ ] Pin **only** Seat Watch to the toolbar.
- [ ] Clear the desktop and turn off Slack/Mail/Messages notifications — a personal message
      sliding in during the alert shot means reshooting the whole take. `Focus → Do Not
      Disturb`, but confirm Chrome's notifications still come through, or you lose the money
      shot too.
- [ ] Set the window to exactly the store's aspect:

```sh
osascript -e 'tell application "Google Chrome" to set bounds of front window to {0, 0, 1280, 800}'
```

- [ ] Have the watch list already populated with 2–3 real shows *before* the take. An empty
      popup is a bad first frame and there's no reason to record the setup.

**Recording**

- `⌘⇧5` → **Record Selected Portion** → drag to the Chrome window, or Options → the window.
  Turn **Show Mouse Pointer** on; the cursor is doing a lot of the explaining.
- Prefer **Kap** or **OBS** if you want click highlights — a visible ring on the + click makes
  that beat land much harder than a bare cursor.
- Record at 60fps if offered. Scrolling BookMyShow at 30fps looks cheap.
- Move the cursor **slowly and deliberately**, and pause a full second before each click.
  Natural mouse speed is unreadable in a demo.
- Do each beat as its own take. Cutting between takes is free; a 60-second single take with
  one fumble at 0:48 is not.

**Editing**

- Captions: one line, large, bottom third, high contrast, 2s minimum. Match the extension's
  green (`#0f7b48`-ish, sample it from the popup) for accent text so the video and the
  product look like the same thing.
- No music under the VO section if you're recording voice — or duck it hard. If you're not
  recording voice, captions must carry every beat above.
- Export: H.264 MP4, 1280×800 for the master. The Chrome Web Store takes a **YouTube URL**,
  not a file, so the master needs to go up on YouTube regardless.
- Keep the raw footage. You'll want a re-cut for every subsequent launch post.

**The one shot worth waiting for**

A real late release, caught live: sold-out chip in the morning, notification at 7pm, seat map
with four together at 7:01. It is the only footage that can't be staged, it will outperform
everything else you shoot, and you can only get it by leaving the extension running on a real
opening weekend. Set it watching a genuinely sold-out Friday show and keep a screen recorder
armed.
